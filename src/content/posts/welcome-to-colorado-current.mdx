Colorado invented community solar. It's home to the deepest geothermal well ever drilled in the state. A Denver startup just raised $400 million to find hydrogen underground. Another in Loveland is replacing 150-year-old industrial boilers with heat pumps.

None of this gets the coverage it deserves.

Colorado Current exists to fix that. Every two weeks I'll publish one of two things: a company spotlight diving deep on a specific Colorado clean energy startup, or an industry analysis piece looking at a trend, policy development, or market dynamic shaping the ecosystem.

Everything is backed by a living directory of 40+ Colorado clean energy companies — tracking their sector, stage, funding, and what makes them interesting. The dashboard on the homepage pulls together ecosystem metrics that don't exist in one place anywhere else.

This isn't a cheerleading publication. Colorado's clean energy transition is genuinely exciting, but it's also complicated — there are companies that will fail, policies that will stall, and bets that won't pay off. I'll cover all of it.

If you work in Colorado clean energy, invest in it, cover it, or just care about it — I hope Colorado Current becomes something you check regularly.
